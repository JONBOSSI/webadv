EURO 2000 EURO 2000 was held in Belgium and the Netherlands. France won the tournament after defeating Italy 2–1 in the final with David Trezeguet scoring the golden goal in extra time. Italy were the runners-up, while Portugal impressed by reaching the semi-finals. Germany were one of the biggest disappointments, finishing last in their group.

EURO 2004 EURO 2004 took place in Portugal and produced one of the biggest surprises in European Championship history. Greece shocked everyone by defeating Portugal 1–0 in the final to win their first major international trophy. Portugal finished as runners-up, while Greece were clearly the tournament's biggest underdog story. Italy were disappointed after being eliminated in the group stage.

EURO 2008 EURO 2008 was hosted by Austria and Switzerland. Spain became champions after beating Germany 1–0 in the final, with Fernando Torres scoring the only goal. Germany were runners-up, while Russia were the surprise team, reaching the semi-finals. France disappointed after finishing bottom of their group.

EURO 2012 EURO 2012 was hosted by Poland and Ukraine. Spain made history by becoming the first team to win three major tournaments in a row, defeating Italy 4–0 in the final. Italy were runners-up, while Greece surprised many by reaching the quarter-finals. The Netherlands were a major disappointment after losing all three group matches.

EURO 2016 EURO 2016 was held in France and featured 24 teams for the first time. Portugal won their first European Championship by defeating hosts France 1–0 after extra time, with Eder scoring the winning goal. France finished as runners-up, while Wales were the underdogs after reaching the semi-finals. England were a major disappointment after being eliminated by Iceland in the round of 16.

EURO 2020 EURO 2020 was postponed to 2021 because of the COVID-19 pandemic and was played across several European cities. Italy won the tournament after defeating England on penalties in the final at Wembley. England finished as runners-up, while Denmark were one of the biggest underdog stories after reaching the semi-finals despite Christian Eriksen's collapse in their opening match. France, the 2018 World Cup champions, disappointed by being eliminated by Switzerland in the round of 16.

EURO 2024 EURO 2024 was hosted by Germany. Spain won their record fourth European Championship after defeating England 2–1 in the final, with Mikel Oyarzabal scoring the winning goal. England were runners-up for the second consecutive EURO. Georgia were the tournament's biggest underdog story, reaching the round of 16 in their first-ever European Championship. Germany, as the host nation, were disappointed after being eliminated by Spain in the quarter-finals.
